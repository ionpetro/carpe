# Carpe research: what goes wrong in the Latin Panhellenic exam, and how to prepare

Written 2026-11-01. Exam: Λατινικά Προσανατολισμού, Γ΄ ΓΕΛ, Ομάδα Ανθρωπιστικών Σπουδών, June 2027 (2027 date not yet announced as far as I found; 2022-2026 papers were all in the first 8 days of June).

## 0. How to read this file (honesty first)

- **No public examiner error report exists, as far as I could find.** I searched Greek and English for παρατηρήσεις βαθμολογητών, ΟΕΦΕ, ΚΕΕ, φροντιστήρια and φιλόλογοι blogs. The ΚΕΕ publishes only indicative answers and instructions to markers, not "what students got wrong". Greek tutor sites publish commentary on the paper (what was hard, what was new), not error statistics. So nothing here says "X% of students lose marks on Y".
- What I did instead: (A) read the **five real papers 2022-2026 in full** (PDFs from alfavita.gr and CNN Greece) and the tutor answer keys; (B) read the ΟΕΦΕ / Διακρότημα / Πουκαμισάς commentary on each year; (C) read the controversies about ΚΕΕ marking; (D) read the Ministry's own 2024-25 teaching-instructions PDF (with the exam-style introduction question bank and the per-lesson etymology list); (E) searched the learning-science literature.
- Labels used below: **[PRIMARY]** read directly from an official paper / ministry document; **[TUTOR]** from a tutor group's commentary or answer key (fallible); **[MY COUNT]** my own tally of the five papers; **[INFERENCE]** my reasoning; **[GENERAL]** standard Latin/learning knowledge not tied to a Greek source I opened (verify before it goes in the app).
- Anything I could not verify is listed in section 9.

## 1. The exam, exactly [PRIMARY: papers 2022-2026]

All five papers have the same skeleton (marks sum to 100):

| Part | What | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Α1 | Translate two excerpts (chosen from a 14-16 line set passage; 10-12 lines are asked) | 20 |
| Β1 | Introduction (literature history) closed-type question | 10 |
| Β2 | Etymology / vocabulary: link Latin words with Greek words of the same root | 10 |
| Γ1 | Grammar: nouns, adjectives, pronouns, adverbs: asked forms | 15 |
| Γ2 | Grammar: verbs: asked forms (Γ2α find+transform in the text, Γ2β list of forms) | 15 |
| Δ1 | Syntax: identify the syntactic role of 5-6 items (Δ1α) + one more task (Δ1β) | 15 |
| Δ2 | Syntax: transformations / clause analysis (Δ2α, Δ2β) | 15 |

So **grammar + syntax = 60 of 100 marks** and translation is only 20. (Confirmed by alfavita and by the papers themselves.) Duration 3 hours. Instruction on every paper: "Κάθε απάντηση τεκμηριωμένη είναι αποδεκτή" (every documented answer is accepted), blue or black pen only, do not copy the questions, notes on the paper are not marked.

The set passages come from textbook lessons XVI-L (Πασχάλης-Σαββαντίδης, Λατινικά τ. Α΄ XVI-XX and τ. Β΄ XXI-L; reference grammar Τζάρτζανος) [PRIMARY: 2021 ministry letter 75296/Δ2]. Which lessons were examined (from Διακρότημα commentary, [TUTOR]): 2022 texts 16, 36, 45; 2024 texts 19, 42; 2025 texts 24, 39; 2026 texts 35, 49. So any lesson from 16 to 50 can appear. 2023 I did not get the text numbers for.

## 2. What tutors and the federation say was hard each year [TUTOR]

- **2022** (Διακρότημα / Πουκαμισάς via CNN, alfavita): Introduction and etymology were tested for the first time, both as matching, which the teacher said helped "since students found the introduction hard all year". Hardest task: the **verb exercise (irregular, deponent, -io verbs)**; first grammar exercise = pronouns, comparatives, a mix of declensions. Syntax: "expected and manageable".
- **2023**: not found.
- **2024** (Πουκαμισάς, Μαρία Γκυρτή, CNN): "οι ερωτήσεις της γραμματικής και του συντακτικού ήταν ιδιαίτερα απαιτητικές", harder than 2023, for well-prepared students. Διακρότημα (Γιαννάκης) said easy/as expected. **So the two big groups disagreed**; take both as noise around "grammar and syntax decide the mark".
- **2025** (ΟΕΦΕ): clear, no special difficulty; Β1 detailed T/F; Δ2α indirect-to-direct not asked for 4-5 years; Δ2β active-to-passive "αρκετά απαιτητική" because **9 words** had to change.
- **2026** (ΟΕΦΕ): clear; syntax had "παγίδες - αστοχίες":
  - **Carthago (Δ1β)**: the textbook (text 25) uses it both as a city and as a wider territory, with and without a preposition. Candidates and teachers objected that more than one reading was possible. The Διακρότημα key gave Carthagine (3rd decl. city name, bare ablative).
  - **Δ2β γ**: the question asked for "εμπρόθετη γενική του γερουνδίου" but because **gerundive attraction is obligatory** with a transitive gerund + object, the real answer is eius obiurgandae causa (the pre-attraction form eam obiurgandi causa is what Διακρότημα wrote in brackets). ΟΕΦΕ: may confuse students; teachers said it admitted "περισσότερες από μία επιστημονικά τεκμηριωμένες απαντήσεις".
  - **Γ2β vēnit**: the question did not say active or passive; ΟΕΦΕ said it should have.
- **ΟΕΦΕ's repeated summary**: well-prepared students, who treated the course "με σοβαρότητα και ψυχραιμία" and studied consistently all year, can get top marks (also headline of an InfoKids piece: "απαιτούσαν συνέπεια όλη τη χρονιά").

## 3. Marking behaviour that costs marks [mostly TUTOR, one teacher's account]

From Γιώργος Πλακίδας (Latin teacher, study4exams), alfavita, 13 Jun 2025: https://www.alfavita.gr/panellinies/480698_peri-latinikon-panelladikon-kee-kai-allon-daimonion (an **opinion piece**, but it reports specific ΚΕΕ decisions):
- In 2025 Δ2α two near-identical indirect-speech sentences were scored twice.
- The ΚΕΕ marker instructions did not give marks for the **question mark** in direct speech; after many marking centres asked, ΚΕΕ said it was apparently required. Lesson: **write the "?" when turning an indirect question into a direct one.**
- Δ2α ii (Exclamāvit Nasīca se domi non esse): the author argued that Nasica domi non est / Ego domi non sum / Domi non sum are all valid; ΚΕΕ accepted only Ego domi non sum. Lesson [INFERENCE]: in conversion tasks, write the pronoun subject (ego) the textbook way, even though Latin can drop it.
- Δ2β (active to passive): sentence had four subjects; one point wrong costs several marks; ΚΕΕ later ruled that a prepositional agent for the inanimate agents is also correct. The marker-level rule is volatile.
- The ΕΚΠΑ document I found (https://eclass.uoa.gr/... "Σφάλματα στην αξιολόγηση") reports that among papers marked independently by five markers, **Latin had a mean score difference of 2.97 marks out of 20** (maximum difference 12), second only to Έκθεση and Φιλοσοφία (per search summary; I did not open the PDF myself, treat as [UNVERIFIED]).

Marker-friendly habits, supported by the official instructions and the answer keys [INFERENCE from PRIMARY]:
1. Answer in the notebook, do not copy questions, one colour of pen.
2. "Κάθε απάντηση τεκμηριωμένη είναι αποδεκτή": **justify** (give the rule name) whenever the task is open: marks per component (see 4.4).
3. When a form has several textbook-valid variants (e.g. abl sg -e/-i, perf 3pl -erunt/-ere), give the textbook primary form; if the instruction says "με όλους τους δυνατούς τρόπους" (2022 Δ2α), give all patterns.

## 4. Trap catalogue, built from the papers and keys

### 4.1 Reading the question (the cheapest marks)
All of these are wording features that appear in the real papers:
- **"στον ίδιο / τον άλλο αριθμό", "στο ίδιο / άλλο γένος", "στην ίδια φωνή"**: a single missed word gives a correct-looking wrong answer (e.g. 2022 ille: both nom neuter AND gen fem; 2023 fuerat: same form in subjunctive).
- **"τους άλλους ΔΥΟ βαθμούς"** (the paper bolds **δύο**) vs "τον συγκριτικό" vs "τους τρεις βαθμούς": 2024 multi asks for **three** degrees of the **adverb**.
- **"επιρρήματος" attached to an adjective form** (2026 certissimum: the answer is the positive adverb certe; 2025 optimum: the comparative adverb melius). Students must first change part of speech.
- **"να ληφθεί υπόψη το υποκείμενο"** (2022 mirāti sunt, 2023 periphrastic): agreement of participles (miraturos esse, miratos fore; fuerint ascensuri).
- **Missing voice** in 2026 (vēnit): when the voice is not stated, check what exists (a verb like venio has no ordinary passive; ΟΕΦΕ thought the question should still have said it).
- Macrons are printed in the exam texts to separate forms (**vēnit** perfect vs **venit** present; **fugit/fūgit**). Carpe should show macrons in texts and never require the student to type them.

### 4.2 Morphology: nouns, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns [forms below are from the tutor answer keys unless marked GENERAL]
- **Genitive plural of 3rd declension**: i-stems take -ium (noctium, urbium, dotium, hostium); consonant stems -um (sororum, vocum); **exception noun "sedes → sedum"** (2023 key). Frequent exam item (3 of 5 papers). Rules for -ium vs -um: GENERAL, verify against Τζάρτζανος. Candidate mnemonic: "parisyllabic in -is/-es and monosyllables with two consonants before the ending are i-stems, exceptions canis, iuvenis, sedes, volucris" [GENERAL, unverified in Greek school terms].
- **Neuter 3rd declension**: ebur voc = nom (**ebur**, 2025); rus acc pl **rura** (2026); pondus nom pl **pondera**; corpus/tempus types.
- **4th declension and "res"**: vultum gen = **vultus** (2022); rem abl = **re**, res ipsa gen = **rei ipsius** (2023).
- **Caedes dative**: **caedi** (2022 key).
- **Pronouns**: ille gen fem **illius**, nom neut **illud** (2022); id gen pl fem **earum** (2024, 2026); quidam gen **cuiusdam** (2024); is, ea, id → eius, eorum; **idem gen pl: eōrundem** [GENERAL] (2025 Eōdem); **mihi ipsi → ego ipse** (2025: the dative of ego + ipse must go to the nominative).
- **Comparative / superlative**: aequum → aequius, aequissimum; magna abl comparative **maiore** (2022); clari → clarior, clarissimus; viliōrem → the other two degrees of vilis; ingentem → ingentiorem, ingentissimum. Superlative -errimus for adjectives in -er (celer → celerrimus, celerrime) and -illimus for -lis group (facilis, difficilis, similis…) are in the official syllabus ("υπερθετικός των επιθέτων σε -er και -lis").
- **Adverbs**: formation from adjectives (certus → certe, apertus → aperte, celer → celeriter); degrees celerius/celerrime, diutius/diutissime, apertius/apertissime; **irregular: bene, melius, optime; multum, plus, plurimum** (2024 key: "multum/multo, plus, plurimum"; the paper says "τους τρεις βαθμούς του επιρρήματος" for multi).

### 4.3 Morphology: verbs
- **Supine every year (5 of 5)**: acc -tum (acceptum, missum), abl -tu (acceptu, motu, intellectu). Students must know the **fourth principal part**. Principal parts are what the whole verb system hangs on: e.g. accipio/accepi/acceptum; moveo/movi/motum; intellego/intellexi/intellectum; mitto/misi/missum; fero/tuli/latum.
- **Imperatives, every year**: dic, duc, fac, fer (no -e); **fac / facito**, **fer / ferto**; compounds keep the pattern (**conferto**, 2026); **fio → fite** (2022: "Fit": 2pl imperative); **abicio → abice** (2022; vowel change from iacio); passive imperative **dicimini** is NOT imperative: the 2023 item asks for the 2pl present PASSIVE indicative, whose form is the same as the passive imperative (Latin -mini).
- **Fero and compounds** (conferre, offero, differo): the principal complexity is fer-/tul-/lat-. A teacher's one-page table exists: https://filologikosxoleio.gr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/fero-tuli-latum-ferre.pdf (I read it: active and passive full paradigm, imperative fer/ferto/ferte/fertote, supine latum/latu, gerund ferendi/ferendo/ferendum/ferendo, future infinitive latum iri (passive), no perfect participle active). Note the table shows the future infinitive passive is latum iri (a trap for students who want latum esse).
- **Eo and compounds**: adeundo (abl gerund of adīre, 2022 key), **redeuntis** (gen sg of the present participle, 2023: the participle of eo declines on the stem euntis), ii/ivi/itum.
- **Gerund vs gerundive** (ΟΕΦΕ 2026 trap; official syllabus items "Το γερούνδιο", "Το γερουνδιακό", "Το ποιητικό αίτιο του γερουνδιακού", "Η γερουνδιακή έλξη και ο προσδιορισμός του σκοπού"): gerund has no nominative and is a noun (cases gen -ndi, dat -ndo, acc -ndum after prepositions, abl -ndo). When the gerund would govern an accusative object, school Latin uses the **gerundive agreeing with the object** (eius obiurgandae causa). Gerundive = passive meaning, adjective, 3 genders.
- **Deponents**: passive form, active meaning; present participle is -ens; future participle -urus; perfect participle (secūtus, indignātus) has ACTIVE meaning. Typical exam items: find deponents (2025: three of them), their present participles nom sg fem (mentiens...), their infinitives, utor → **utens, usurus, usus** (2022 key).
- **Passive vs deponent look-alikes** (2026 Γ2α): elapso → elabi, molientem → moliri (deponents), **vocātus → vocari (a normal passive participle: infinitive "vocari" is the passive infinitive of voco)**. Students who see a passive-looking form must decide "deponent or ordinary passive?".
- **Perfect system of sum / compounds**: possit → potuisset (pluperfect subjunctive), fuerat → fuisset, fore (= futurum esse) → in the 2024 key "esse" for "απαρέμφατο ενεστώτα".
- **Periphrastic conjugation**: active (-urus sum) and passive (-ndus sum). 2023: "Erat ascensūrus → fuerint ascensuri" (subject plural, so the participle is plural); 2025: "agunt → acturus es". The papers instruct "take the subject into account".
- **Subjunctive**: present active 1pl exclamemus, discedamus; passive; plus-perfect aluissemus, venissent. 2022-2026 each had 2 to 3 subjunctive forms in Γ2 and every Δ2/Δ1 mood justification depends on it.
- **Future perfect / future infinitives**: miraturos esse, miratos fore.
- **Parts of speech in forms questions**: a perfect participle "credis → creditum" (neuter nom sg) in 2025 (credere's perfect participle is creditus, -a, -um).

### 4.4 Syntax (Δ1, Δ2): the structure of marks [PRIMARY: papers; templates from TUTOR keys]
- **Δ1α "αναγνώριση συντακτικών όρων"** has a fixed answer pattern "είναι ... στο ...". The key's phrases are terms from the school grammar. Examples taken verbatim from the Διακρότημα keys (not official, but they show the expected granularity):
  - ab eo: εμπρόθετος επιρρηματικός προσδιορισμός του ποιητικού αιτίου στο desertum esse
  - curāri: υποκείμενο (infinitive) του απροσώπου placuit (the key wrote "τελικό απαρέμφατο, υποκείμενο...")
  - semper: επιρρηματικός προσδιορισμός του χρόνου στο exigeret
  - uxor: ομοιόπτωτος προσδιορισμός ως παράθεση στο Porcia
  - temerarium: επιθετικός προσδιορισμός στο factum
  - amōris: ετερόπτωτος προσδιορισμός, γενική αντικειμενική στο indicium
  - se (2023): άμεσο αντικείμενο στο dabat, άμεση αυτοπάθεια
  - standi: γενική του γερουνδίου, γενική αντικειμενική στο mora
  - paulo: απρόθετη αφαιρετική του μέτρου ή της διαφοράς στο post
  - contra Claudium: εμπρόθετος προσδιορισμός της εναντίωσης
  - risu: αφαιρετική οργανική του τρόπου; ob rem: εμπρόθετος του εξωτερικού αναγκαστικού αιτίου
  - ingenii: γενική της ιδιότητας; non credendo: απρόθετη αφαιρετική γερουνδίου που δηλώνει τρόπο; factum esse: ειδικό απαρέμφατο, αντικείμενο στο dicerent; eius: γενική κτητική.
  Lesson: the answer is **form + role + the word it depends on**, using exact school names. The key varies slightly between tutor groups, so Carpe must pick the textbook term (Τζάρτζανος / Συντακτικό) and accept near-synonyms.
- **Mood-justification ("αιτιολογήστε τον τρόπο εκφοράς") is worth 2-4 marks in 5 of 5 papers** and has a stable skeleton in the keys. Examples:
  - Volitive/purpose/consecutive: "εκφέρεται με υποτακτική, γιατί το περιεχόμενο είναι απλώς επιθυμητό (βουλητική) / γιατί το αποτέλεσμα στα λατινικά θεωρείται υποκειμενική κατάσταση (συμπερασματική)"; then "συγκεκριμένα με υποτακτική ενεστώτα/παρατατικού, γιατί εξαρτάται από αρκτικό/ιστορικό χρόνο" and **"ιδιομορφία στην ακολουθία των χρόνων" (συγχρονισμός) for volitive/consecutive/purpose clauses** (2022 ne-clause, 2023 ut-clause, 2024 qui non fateātur).
  - **cum historicum**: subjunctive because it "υπογραμμίζει τη βαθύτερη σχέση ... αιτίου-αιτιατού"; pluperfect subjunctive (cognovisset) because it depends on a historical tense (poposcit) and shows **προτερόχρονο στο παρελθόν** (2026 key).
  - 2023: dum ... audirētur: subjunctive "γιατί η πράξη περιγράφεται ως προσδοκία ή επιδίωξη" (dum = until, expectation).
  - Marks are split by component (2026 Δ2α: type 1, function 1, how introduced 1, justify mood 2, condensation 2). Carpe should drill the components as separate micro-answers.
- **Transformations**:
  - Active ↔ passive: agent handled with **ab + ablative for persons**, bare ablative for things, but KEE accepted the prepositional agent for inanimate in 2025 after a dispute (see 3). The task is long, so errors compound: 2025 needed 9 words changed. Passive of the whole sentence includes **verb person/number agreement with the new subject**, and participle agreement in compound tenses.
  - Direct ↔ indirect: **question mark** (KEE), ego/te/se pronoun shifts, ACI after verbs of saying (scribit se adfore → "Ego adero"), subjunctive in indirect questions (quid Nasīca fecerit → "Quid Nasica fecit?"), sequence of tenses. 2022 Δ2β key: "Ego cum legionibus celeriter adero".
  - Participle to clause (2022): all patterns, using quod / quia / quoniam / cum with the right tense (missum → quod missum erat; veritus → quod veritus erat / esset / cum veritus esset).
  - Clause to participle (2026): the Διακρότημα key gives the ablative absolute viri sui consilio de interficiendo Caesare cognito. [UNCERTAIN] A general rule (e.g. warwick.ac.uk teaching note) says an ablative absolute should not share its subject with the main clause, and here the clause subject (Porcia) is also the subject of the main verb (poposcit); the answer key's choice is therefore surprising. I could not check the school Συντακτικό. Do not turn this into an app rule until it is checked against Τζάρτζανος / the school Συντακτικό.
  - Ablative absolute to cum-clause (2024): Cum Marcus Tullius Cicero et Gaius Antonius consules essent (2024 key), with the label "ιδιόμορφη αφαιρετική απόλυτη που δηλώνει χρόνο; consulibus κατηγορηματικός προσδιορισμός".
  - Conditional (2025): identify "εκφορά και είδος" (indicative or subjunctive and kind of condition: here a past unreal condition) and convert to a possible/probable condition. I do not have the key; verify.
  - Purpose in three ways (2026): ut + subjunctive; supine (eam obiurgatum, after a verb of motion); gerund/gerundive with causa or gratia, with attraction (eius obiurgandae causa).
  - Place expressions (2026): Romae (gen of 1st-decl. singular city names), Athenis (abl pl), Carthagine (abl 3rd decl.); preposition + abl for countries/regions (in Africā). Domi/rure/humi (locative-type forms) are the usual extension [GENERAL].

- **Β2 etymology**: two formats. ## 7. Learning science that matches this exam [mixed: see labels]

Overlaps with /RESEARCH.md for Skepsis (Dunlosky 2013, Rowland 2014, spaced retrieval, interleaving, Duolingo streak data). New items checked for Carpe:
- **Testing effect**: Roediger & Karpicke 2006, Psychological Science 17(3): initial tests beat restudy on delayed tests (2 days, 1 week) though restudy won at 5 minutes. Supports retrieval-first cards. https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf
- **Optimal spacing depends on how far away the test is**: Cepeda et al. 2008, Psychological Science 19(11) "Spacing Effects in Learning: A Temporal Ridgeline of Optimal Retention": optimal gap was about 20-40% of a 1-week test delay and about 5-10% of a 1-year test delay (so the ratio falls as the retention interval rises). https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf. **[INFERENCE for Carpe]**: the exam is about 7 months (~210 days) away, so the first review gaps should be days, later gaps weeks (Leitner 1/3/7/14/30 is reasonable; add a 45-60-day box close to the exam). Do not claim more precision.
- **Interleaving in verb morphology**: Pan, Tajran, Lovelett, Osuna, Rickard (2018 preprint/2019): learners of Spanish verb conjugation; interleaving did NOT help within one session but **did help when practice was spread across multiple sessions**; a later study with two Romance languages found interleaving improved conjugation in all experiments (Pan et al. 2024). The journal reference for the first paper I could not confirm. https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PTLOR_2019W.pdf . Supports mixing tenses/moods/declensions across days.
- **Spaced vs massed for grammar**: mixed; a classroom study of English-speaking learners of French found little difference between 7-day and 3.5-day spacing for a verb inflection subsystem (search summary, [WEAK]).
- **Worked examples**: I did not find Latin-specific studies; the "worked example then fade" idea is GENERAL (cognitive load literature: Sweller). Use it for syntax transformations: show a solved transformation, then a partially solved one, then a blank one. [GENERAL/UNVERIFIED]
- **Test anxiety / stress**: Jamieson, Mendes, Blackstock, Schmader 2010, J. Exp. Social Psychology 46(1), "Turning the knots in your stomach into bows": students told that arousal helps performance did better on practice GRE math and on the real GRE 1-3 months later (small study; the authors note it applies to motivated-performance situations). https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/4214916 . Ramirez & Beilock 2011, Science 331: writing about exam worries for 10 minutes before a high-stakes test raised performance, mainly in high-anxiety students; field study with 9th-grade biology; a published letter (Lang & Lang) questioned the practical advice and said it might hurt low-anxiety students. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/01/110113141605.htm
  - **Use in Carpe [INFERENCE]**: an optional 2-minute "write the worry" and a "my heart is racing = my body is getting ready" line in the pre-exam countdown week. Keep it optional, label it as a tip, never as treatment.
- **Gamification and streaks**: see RESEARCH.md rows (Sailer & Homner 2020; Duolingo streak/freeze data; engagement is not learning, LMU study; hard streaks can cause stress). Keep "no hearts, free freezes".

## 8. What this means for the app (rules derived from the evidence)

1. **Mirror the paper.** Offer a "Θέμα Γ1 / Γ2 / Δ1 / Δ2 / Β1 / Β2" mode with the **real wording** ("στον άλλο αριθμό", "τους άλλους δύο βαθμούς", "στην ίδια φωνή") and the real marks per component. Weight practice by marks: grammar+syntax 60%, translation 20%, introduction 10%, etymology 10%.
2. **Make every drilled form carry its principal parts.** Supine and imperatives appear every year; therefore store 4 principal parts for every verb and test supine and imperative separately.
3. **Question-reading trainer**: a mini-game that only asks "what exactly is requested?" (highlight the instruction words ίδιο/άλλο, δύο/τρεις, φωνή, γένος) before answering.
4. **Mood-justification builder** (2-4 marks, 5 of 5 papers): tile-build the sentence from the skeleton (type, function, conjunction, mood + why, tense + why, sequence-of-tenses peculiarity).
5. **Transformation lab** for the rotating syntax tasks: active↔passive, direct↔indirect (with "?"), participle↔clause, ablative absolute↔cum, purpose in 3 ways, place expressions, conditionals. Worked example, then faded.
6. **Introduction bank**: load the Ministry's 27 open + 73 T/F + closed items; 10 marks are close to free. Plus textbook-only extras (Tibullus, Georgics...).
7. **Etymology both ways** (cognates and Romance loans) with distractors; teach the root, not the pair.
8. **Past-paper mode**: the five real papers 2022-2026 as timed mocks, scored by component. **Check copyright/permission before reproducing the paper text** (Ministry documents; textbook texts are from ΙΤΥΕ Διόφαντος books). [UNVERIFIED]
9. **Cross-check content with two sources**: tutor keys disagree and ΚΕΕ itself changed accepted answers in 2025, so each answer in Carpe should have a "δεκτή εναλλακτική" field and be reviewed against Τζάρτζανος. Never invent a rule that the textbook does not state (e.g. the 2026 ablative-absolute key, see 4.4).
10. **Calm-down design** for a stressed 17-year-old: a countdown to June 2027 with "minimum day" (5 minutes keeps the streak), a weekly mock, a mistake notebook that feeds the next sessions (cearsplus tip + spaced retrieval), optional worry-writing in the last week.
